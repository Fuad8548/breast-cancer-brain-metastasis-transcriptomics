# Microarray vs. RNA-Seq
We used `limma` because **GSE25066** is a Microarray dataset, which means using DESeq2 here is statistically impossible.

Bioinformatics tools are completely dependent on the mathematical nature of the raw data generated by the sequencing machine.
- Why `DESeq2` Fails on Microarrays: DESeq2 is strictly designed for RNA-Seq read counts. RNA-Seq counting yields discrete integers (0, 1, 14, 520), which DESeq2 models using a **Negative Binomial distribution**. If we pass continuous, log-transformed microarray values into `DESeq2`, the package will immediately crash or throw an error stating it requires non-negative integers.
- Why `limma` Excels Here: NCBI **GSE25066** was generated using the **Affymetrix Human Genome U133A Microarray**. Microarrays do not count reads; they measure the intensity of fluorescent light. This produces continuous numerical data that follows a Normal (Gaussian) distribution after log-transformation. limma (Linear Models for Microarray Data) was custom-engineered to handle this continuous distribution via linear modeling and Empirical Bayes moderation.


# Interpretation of the result

## Understanding the biomarkers table
In R’s `limma` package, a negative `logFC` indicates higher expression in Resistant (RD) tumors strictly because limma calculated the mathematical difference as `Sensitive (pCR) − Resistant (RD)`.

1. logFC (Log2 Fold Change) — Effect Size & Direction:
	• Significance: Tells us which group is higher (- = Resistant RD, + = Sensitive pCR) and how big the jump is ($\(2^{|{}\text{logFC}|{}}\)$). Used to draw the X-axis of our Volcano Plot.
2. AveExpr (Average Log2 Expression) — Wet-Lab Detectability Filter:
	• Significance: Tells us whether a gene is actually abundant enough to measure in a real lab! Look at row 319 in our file (210683_at): it has logFC = -1.29, but its AveExpr is only 3.10 (barely above background noise). In contrast, TMSB15A (AveExpr = 9.00) and TTLL4 (AveExpr = 7.08) are strongly expressed and easy to detect by RT-qPCR or RNA-seq.
3. t (Moderated t-Statistic) — Signal-to-Noise Ratio & GSEA Ranking:
	• Significance: Combines logFC with patient-to-patient consistency (logFC / Standard Error). In further studies, bioinformaticians use the t column to run Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) because it penalizes noisy genes with high variance.
4. P.Value (Nominal P-value) — Raw Single-Test Probability:
	• Significance: Shows the raw probability before testing 22,283 probes. Never used alone for publication because testing 22,000 probes produces ~1,100 false positives by pure chance at P < 0.05.
5. adj.P.Val (Benjamini-Hochberg FDR) — Publication Significance Filter:
	• Significance: Protects us from false discoveries across 22,283 tests. us used adj.P.Val < 0.05 to filter down to our 2,938 true biomarkers, and -log10(adj.P.Val) forms the Y-axis of our Volcano Plot.
6. B (Log-Odds of Differential Expression) — Bayesian Confidence Score:
	• Significance: Gives the log-odds that a gene is genuinely differentially expressed rather than a statistical fluke. Whenever B > 0 (for example, TTLL4 has B = 17.54 and TMSB15A has B = 16.84), the probability that the gene is truly altered exceeds 99.9999%.


## Part 1: Theoretical Statistics & Interpretation of the Axes
A volcano plot acts as a visual filter. It combines two completely independent statistical metrics to separate meaningful biological signals from random genetic background noise.

1. **The Horizontal Axis**: $\(\log _{2}\)$ Fold Change 
- What it measures: The magnitude and direction of the expression shift. It answers the question: By how much is a gene turned up or down in resistant tumors compared to sensitive ones?
- The Math: Fold change is converted to a $\(\log _{2}\)$ scale to make variations symmetrical.
	- A $\(\log_2\text{FC}\)$ of +1 means the gene is expressed $\(2^1 = 2\text{-fold}\)$ higher in resistant tumors (Upregulated).
	- A $\(\log_2\text{FC}\)$ of +2 means the gene is expressed $\(2^2 = 4\text{-fold}\)$ higher (Highly Upregulated).
	- A $\(\log_2\text{FC}\)$ of -1 means the gene is expressed $\(2^{-1} = 0.5\)$ or $\(2\text{-fold}\)$ lower in resistant tumors (Downregulated).

2. The Vertical Axis: $\(-\log _{10}\)$ Raw P-Value
- What it measures: The statistical certainty or reproducibility of the change. It answers the question: How confident are we that this difference isn't a random fluke?
- The Math: Raw p-values fall between 0 and 1 (where numbers close to 0 mean high significance). Transforming them using a negative logarithm makes the numbers intuitive to plot:
	- If a gene has a p-value of 0.01, $\(-\log_{10}(0.01) = \mathbf{2}\)$.
	- If a gene has a p-value of 0.00001, $\(-\log_{10}(0.00001) = \mathbf{5}\)$.
	- Rule of thumb: The higher up a dot sits on the vertical axis, the more statistically bulletproof its variation is.


# Part 2: Understanding the Dashed Line Thresholds
The dashed lines establish the boundary rules for our project, separating the plot into quadrants:
1. The Vertical Dashed Lines ($\(\pm 0.5\)$): These mark our biological effect size cutoffs. Any gene falling between -0.5 and +0.5 shifts too weakly to be considered a strong biological driver, even if it is statistically reliable.
2. The Horizontal Dashed Line (around 2.3): This represents our raw p-value equivalent to our false discovery rate threshold (**adj.P.Val < 0.05**).
3. The Resulting Quadrants:
	- Grey Dots (Bottom & Center): Genes that fail either the statistical test, the magnitude test, or both. These are classified as **uninformative genetic noise**.
	- Red Dots (Top Right Quadrant): Transcripts that pass the p-value threshold and have a $\(\log_2\text{FC} > 0.5\)$. These are our **active Resistance Drivers**.
	- Blue Dots (Top Left Quadrant): Transcripts that pass the p-value threshold and have a $\(\log_2\text{FC} < -0.5\)$. These are our **active Sensitivity Drivers** (genes suppressed in resistant tumors).


# Part 3: Biological Significance & Translational Insights
Looking closely at our generated plot reveals a crucial structural feature: The plot is highly balanced and densely populated.
- Symmetrical Red and Blue Wings: We have a clean distribution of both highly upregulated oncogenic pathways (Red) and heavily suppressed protective pathways (Blue). This indicates that chemoresistance is not driven by a single isolated mutation. Instead, it involves a widespread transcriptomic reprogramming of the tumor cells.
- **Highly Certain Outliers (The top-most dots)**: Noticing the single red dot sitting near the top right around $\(\log_2\text{FC} = 1.5\)$, and the blue dot near the top left around $\(\log_2\text{FC} = -1.3\)$. These dots have a $\(-\log_{10}\text{p-value}\)$ above 10. This translates to a raw p-value of less than $\(1 \times 10^{-10}\)$. These specific outliers represent our absolute highest-confidence therapeutic targets.
